# Example: Electronic keyboard from zero

## Known learner goals and confirmed questions
The target is **playing an electronic keyboard**, not professional conservatory piano. Learner describes themselves as a beginner. Early lessons addressed the note names **C D E F G A B**, solfège **Do Re Mi Fa Sol La Ti**, white-key mapping and locating keys quickly, and right-hand finger numbers **1–5 from thumb to little finger**. Key clarification: finger numbers are permanent labels for fingers, **but a given finger is not permanently attached to a fixed note**; finger choice depends on hand position, phrase and technique.

## Proposed stages
1. Keyboard layout, groups of black keys, C–B mapping, solfège, basic rhythm, posture, finger numbering.
2. Five-finger patterns, pulse, note durations, easy right-hand melodies.
3. Left-hand patterns, simple bass/accompaniment and coordination.
4. Reading simple notation/chord symbols, scales, intervals, common chords.
5. Chord progressions, rhythm/style patterns, simple ear training and practical songs.
6. Repertoire, expressive playing, independent practice and self-correction.

## Lesson `S01-M01-L01` continuation example
Ask the learner to locate C, E, G and B in **two different octaves**; identify how keyboard black-key groups provide anchors; play C-D-E-F-G using right-hand 1–5 in a *specific five-finger position*; then change the hand position to disprove the idea that the thumb always plays C.

## Gate
Learner demonstrates correct identification without prompting, steady pulse, clean hand movement and a short complete melody. If real instrument audio or video is unavailable, do not claim to have verified the physical performance; instead ask for a recording or limit judgment to written theory.

**No invented practice completion.** Track actual exercises and student-submitted evidence.

# Example: Systematic Chinese history training

## Position
Chinese history was among the prior long-term training directions. Specific approved chronological boundaries, interpretation goals and baseline are **not validated by this example**; start with clarification and targeted diagnostic rather than pretending to import a fixed prior course.

## Provisional stages
1. Build a chronological overview and the geographical/institutional context.
2. Early China, Warring States, Qin–Han: institutions, state formation, evidence.
3. Disunion, Sui–Tang: transformations, economy, religion, exchange.
4. Song–Yuan: institutions, commerce, society, Eurasian setting.
5. Ming–Qing: governance, culture, global interactions and change.
6. Late Qing, Republic, modern China: competing interpretations and sources.
7. Thematic comparisons: political economy, institutions, technology, geography, historiography.

## Core historical competencies
Chronology; separating event/cause/interpretation; distinguishing primary sources, retrospective narratives and current scholarship; evaluating uncertainty; interpreting evidence without presentism.

## Diagnostic and first assignment
Ask the learner to order 6 periods/events and explain one causal link with uncertainty. Then give two conflicting short historical claims and ask what sources would adjudicate them. Require a timeline and a supported, revised short essay for the first module Gate.

**Avoid** describing contested interpretations as settled facts or manufacturing primary-source quotations.
